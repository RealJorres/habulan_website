The game

A game of chase. A race against time. A curse that never ends.

HABULAN is a multiplayer 3D tag game inspired by the Filipino childhood game of habulan, reimagined as a supernatural mystery in a forgotten island village.

Every player starts with the same time on their own clock, and it only runs while you are the Taya. Tag someone to pass the curse and stop your clock. When a Taya's clock reaches 0:00, the Fifth Bell rings and the match ends. Whoever kept the most time wins.

  • 3–8Players a match
  • 5:00On every clock, online
  • 1 TayaMost time left wins

Gameplay

Run. Hide. Pass it on.

Four beats of every match, shown in real captures from the game.

I

The Chase

Run, evade and outmanoeuvre the others across the village. Rooftops, balconies and stairs are routes too: break the Taya's line of sight and slip away.

  • Sprint, slide, jump and crouch
  • Footsteps carry: hear who is coming
  • First or third person, your choice in practice
II

Become the Taya

The curse has chosen you. The Taya turns into a blue ghost, runs a little faster, and its clock is running. Every second you hold the curse is a second you lose.

  • Only the Taya's clock runs
  • The Taya is 5% faster
  • Every Taya has the Fifth Toll: nearby Runners slow down
III

Pass the Curse

Get within reach, face them and tag. Your clock stops the moment it lands, and theirs begins.

  • Tag from 2 m, with a clear line: walls block a tag
  • No tag-backs: they have to chase someone else
  • Two powerup slots and one character ability
IV

Survive the Fifth Bell

A bell tolls as a Taya's clock runs down, and each one makes that Taya stronger. In the last minute of the lowest clock, the bright day sinks to dusk and fog rolls in.

At 0:00 the broken bell rings, and that player is drawn down into the fog.

The world of HABULAN

Baryo SawiA walled seaside village

A village in the middle of its fiesta: the plaza and its fountain, the market, sari-sari lane, the basketball court, the church on its terrace, the bahay na bato and the pantalan. Then the curse comes, and the day falls.

Around the village

The lore

Some bellsshould never ring.

Long ago, a village by the sea found a bell that belonged to no church.

  1. I

    The Forgotten Village Ang Nakalimutang Pamayanan

    San Isidro del Silencio stood where the road met the sea. Beneath its old stone belfry, one dry season, the people found a bell that belonged to no church. No map remembers the town now.

  2. II

    The Ancient Bell Ang Pinagmulan ng Sumpa

    The villagers rang it to undo their mistakes: a boat that should not have sunk, a fever that should have broken. Each answered prayer borrowed time from another life, and the debt grew heavier than the bell.

  3. III

    The Keeper of Time Ang Tagabantay ng Oras

    When the bell asked for a guardian, a traveller no one could name stepped forward and became the first Taya. Year by year his memories wore away behind a mask, until one word was left to him: Takbo.

  4. IV

    The Five Tollings Ang Limang Tunog

    The first toll put out every candle. The second called the fog up from the sea. The third stopped the clocks. The fourth set the shadows walking. At the fifth, San Isidro del Silencio was gone.

  5. V

    The Curse That Remains Ang mga Nawalang Bantay

    The Keeper was not the last. Each time the mark passes from hand to hand, another runner carries the curse for a while. Some pass it on. Some hear the fifth bell first, and walk into the fog without looking back.

    Ikaw naman ang taya.

A work of fiction. San Isidro del Silencio, its bell and its Keeper are invented. They are inspired by Filipino island life, but they are not Marinduque history or a Moriones tradition.
